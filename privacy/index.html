Student privacy

Your work is yours.

Walsh Classroom is a set of educational apps for school media arts classes. This policy says what student information the site keeps, who can see it, and how to have it removed. It's written to be read by students, families and schools.

Effective November 17, 2026. For schools and IT staff →

The short version

  • No ads, no tracking, no selling. There is no advertising, no analytics or tracking code, and student information is never sold, rented or traded.
  • Only what the apps need. An account is a name, an email address and a password. Apps that save work keep that work in the student's account.
  • Only for school. Student information is used to run the apps for teaching and learning, and for nothing else: no marketing, no profiles, no training AI.
  • The school is in charge. When a school uses Walsh Classroom, student information stays under the school's control, and I'll sign the school's student data privacy agreement.
  • You can see it, fix it or delete it. Email help@walshclassroom.com.

Who runs this site

Walsh Classroom is made and run by Tom Walsh, a media arts teacher, for his own classes and for other teachers who want to use it. It's free, and it has no investors, sponsors or partners. Questions about privacy go to help@walshclassroom.com.

Who it's for

The apps are made for middle and high school students using them for class, and for their teachers. Students under 13 should only make an account when their school has approved Walsh Classroom; the school gives consent for school use, as the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) allows. Anyone can open the apps that don't need an account, such as Guide Match, without giving any information.

What the site collects

When someone makes an account

  • Their name (as they type it) and email address.
  • A password. It's handled by Google's sign-in service (Firebase Authentication) and stored only in scrambled (hashed) form. I can't see anyone's password.
  • Whether the account is a student, teacher or administrator account.

When a student uses the apps

  • Classes: which classes they've joined with a class code, and when.
  • Their work: what they make in apps that save to an account. Today that's Narrative Studio: projects, characters, outlines, scenes and writing.
  • Feedback: comments a teacher leaves on their work, and their replies.
  • Sharing: which projects they've chosen to share with their class.

What's stored on the device, not by the site

  • The light or dark theme choice, and the sign-in session, in the browser's own storage.
  • Projects saved from Virtual Claymation Studio, which save as files on the device.

What the site doesn't collect

No location, no contacts, no photos or recordings, no birth dates, no student ID numbers, no grades, and no browsing history. Skill builders like Guide Match save nothing at all.

How the information is used

Only to provide the apps: signing in, saving and loading work, connecting students to their classes, letting teachers give feedback, and answering help requests. It is never used for advertising, never used to build a profile of a student for any other purpose, never used to train artificial intelligence, and never sold or shared for anyone else's use.

Who can see a student's information

  • The student.
  • Their teacher, for each class the student joins. A teacher can see the work of students in their own classes, and only theirs. Leaving a class, or being removed from it, ends that access.
  • Classmates see a project only if the student shares it with the class. Class rosters are visible to the teacher only.
  • Me, as the site's administrator, only to keep the site running, fix problems, answer a help request, or when the law requires it.

These limits are enforced by the database's security rules, not only by the apps' screens.

Services the site relies on

Walsh Classroom uses a few service providers to work. Each one only handles what it needs to do its job, under its own privacy and security terms:

  • Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore): accounts, passwords and saved work.
  • Google Fonts: the site's typefaces. The browser downloads them from Google.
  • GitHub Pages: hosts the site's pages and apps.
  • Cloudflare: runs the site's domain name, and forwards email sent to help@walshclassroom.com.

No other company receives student information.

Schools and FERPA

When a school or teacher uses Walsh Classroom with students, the student information belongs to the school and is part of the student's education record. I act as a "school official" under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA): I use student information only for the school's purposes, under the school's direction, and don't disclose it except as this policy and the law allow. Schools can have a signed student data privacy agreement, including the Massachusetts and National Student Data Privacy Agreements. See For schools.

Keeping it safe

  • Every page and app loads over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).
  • Passwords are stored only in hashed form by Google, and never by me.
  • Database security rules decide who can read or change each piece of information, and they're kept in the site's public code so anyone can check them.
  • The database's settings and security rules can only be changed from the site owner's Google account.

If student information is ever accessed by someone who shouldn't have it, I'll tell the affected schools and families promptly, say what happened and what's being done, and follow the notice rules in any agreement with the school and in the law.

How long it's kept, and deleting it

Information is kept while the account is in use. A student, a parent or guardian, a teacher, or a school can ask for an account and its work to be deleted, and I'll delete it within 30 days and confirm when it's done. When a school's agreement ends, I'll delete or return that school's student information as the agreement says. Students can also remove a project themselves at any time, and leaving a class removes them from its roster.

Families' rights

Parents and guardians, and students 18 and older, can ask to see the information the site keeps about a student, to correct it, or to delete it. Email help@walshclassroom.com, or go through the student's school. I'll confirm the request comes from the student's family or school before sharing anything.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. Student information will never be used in a new way without notice to schools, and the school's or family's consent where the law or an agreement requires it.
